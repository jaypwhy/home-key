The Framework

How every seed cohort runs: free base needs, regenerative income by choice, and absolute protections — for working and non-working members alike.

The two participation models

Both models receive the whole six-pillar system. They are shown side by side because they are equal in worth, equal in housing, and equal in support.

Working model

Base needs and universal income are fully covered — and regenerative-income pathways are active on top.

  • Skills assessment: we start with what you already know and want to build — not with what you lack on paper.
  • Free training, gamified where possible, for regenerative-economy roles: regenerative agriculture, waste-to-resource, community gardens, ecological restoration, local craft, and digital literacy.
  • Earn-to-learn stipends while training — learning is paid, so building a future never means going without now.
  • Micro-enterprise support with seed funding and mentorship, for members who want to start something of their own.
  • Equitable digital-economy access: hardware, internet, and training are provided. Access is equity, not just an invitation.

Non-working model

Base needs and universal income are fully covered, with no work expectation at all — for disability, caregiving, acute stabilization, or simply choice.

  • Identical housing quality — the same homes, the same standards, the same dignity.
  • Identical cleaning and connectivity rights, held as permanent budget lines that are never removed or reduced.
  • Identical wellness-animal and pet care support.
  • Identical stipends.

Non-participation is respected as autonomy — never punished, never conditional, never framed as "soon to be cut."

Movement between models is free, both directions, any time. A person is never worth less for resting.

Zero-barrier onboarding to universal income

The burden of paperwork belongs to the program, not to the person who needs the money.

  • Automatic enrollment (opt-out)

    Universal income is the default, not a prize. The team does the identifying and the paperwork — not the participant. Nobody has to prove they are poor enough to deserve it.

  • Direct and regular disbursement

    Monthly by default, with a weekly option if that works better for you. Money lands directly with the person, on a schedule they can plan around.

  • Optional support, never a gate

    Non-judgmental financial-literacy workshops and peer mentoring are offered and always opt-in — never mandatory, never condescending.

Framework, not commitment: these are the rules we are designing to, and they are not yet operating on anyone's behalf.

Restorative economic justice

Homelessness in San Diego is not an accident. This framework is built to restore what was taken, not to hand out what was spare.

  • Documented 20+ year displacement is a prioritization tier — race-neutral. Where a household can document two decades or more of displacement, that history moves them forward in line. It is never a test anyone has to pass.
  • Guaranteed contribution pathways that value lived experience without formal qualifications — life experience counts as expertise, and there is a paid place for it.
  • Equitable access to community-managed resources: shared tools, garden land, and workshops, governed by the community that uses them.

Framework, not commitment: prioritization and contribution pathways are ratified direction, and the team is still developing how they will run in practice.

Absolute and non-negotiable

Sacred protections

These four protections are unconditional program rules — not aspirations, and not subject to budget. They bind us before our first participant arrives.

  • Sacred privacy

    Zero surveillance of persons, families, or spiritual and religious life. Data minimization: nothing is stored or tracked beyond what the individual explicitly opts into. Participant stories never become marketing content.

  • No profit from injury or passing

    No monetization of any person's injury, death, or story — by staff, partners, or media, ever. Celebrating contributions never becomes exploiting them.

  • No leveraging family

    No member's support is ever contingent on another member's behavior or compliance. Family reunification happens by choice only.

  • No career-building off others' harm

    No one advances a career or reputation by causing harm to participants. Every staff member and partner signs this code before working with participants.

Unconditional today: these protections are the one part of this page that is not waiting on funding, launch, or scale.

Reintegration principles

Operating rules, not a values poster. Each one describes something a cohort does, or it does not go on this page.

  • Co-design seats

    Participants hold real votes in cohort governance — not advisory seats, real ones.

  • Voluntary participation, genuinely

    Every activity, workshop, and program can be declined without penalty or consequence.

  • Bridge builders

    Community liaisons trusted by the group, chosen with the group, to carry concerns both ways.

  • Equal terms in shared space

    Staff and participants share the same rooms, tables, and programs — no separate tier.

  • Public celebration of contributions

    What people give to their community is named and honored, loudly and accurately.

  • Continuous feedback loops

    Monthly participant sessions, and every change made is publicly attributed to the people who asked for it.

Framework, not commitment: no cohort is running yet, so these rules are written but not yet exercised. The first cohort will report honestly on how they work in practice.

The 12-cohort structure

Project Home Key's Seed Program starts 12 small starter cohorts of 5–25 participants each, planted in neighborhoods across the City of San Diego, each running the same whole six-pillar system described on this page. The first cohort gates the rest: nothing scales until the first seed shows real results, and each wave is reported honestly whether or not targets are met. Phase 0 today means no cohort has launched, no participants are housed, and no funds are raised. Read the full structure on the Seed Program page.
